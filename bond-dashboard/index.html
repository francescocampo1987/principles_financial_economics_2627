2. Duration and convexity

Present value of a cash flow
PVk = CFk / (1 + y/m)m·tk
Price
P = Σ PVk = Σ CFk / (1 + y/m)m·tk
Macaulay duration
DM = Σ tk · PVk / P
Modified duration
Dmod = DM / (1 + y/m)
Convexity
Conv = (1/P) · d²P/dy² = Σ tk (tk + 1/m) · PVk / [P · (1 + y/m)²]

where

tk
number of years between the valuation date and the date on which the cash flow CFk is paid
CFk
cash flow number k: the coupon, plus the par value at maturity
PVk
present value of the cash flow CFk: what it is worth on the valuation date, once discounted over tk years at the yield to maturity
n
number of cash flows
y
annual yield to maturity, as a decimal
m
number of coupon payments (and of compounding periods) per year

3. Price change after a change in yields

Exact
ΔP/P = [P(y + Δy) − P(y)] / P(y)
Duration only
ΔP/P ≈ − Dmod · Δy
Duration and convexity
ΔP/P ≈ − Dmod · Δy + ½ · Conv · (Δy)²

In the formulas Δy is a decimal: 1 percentage point = 0.01 = 100 basis points. Every bond receives the same change in its own yield.

Assumptions: the yield to maturity y is an annual rate compounded as often as the coupon is paid (m times a year); coupons are equally spaced and the bond is valued on a coupon date, just after any payment (no accrued interest); maturity is therefore a whole number of coupon periods; no default risk and no embedded options. A zero-coupon bond is a bond with a coupon rate of 0%.
